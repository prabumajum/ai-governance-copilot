# Regional AI rules: India, UAE, USA

## India
India has no standalone AI law. The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025 govern AI that uses personal data; most obligations apply from 13 May 2027. Key points: no "legitimate interests" basis, so consent or a listed legitimate use is needed; itemized purpose-specific notices; erasure when consent is withdrawn; verifiable parental consent for children's data; and breach reporting with a full report within 72 hours. Significant data fiduciaries must run annual data protection impact assessments and check that algorithms do not harm people's rights. CERT-In directions require reporting cyber incidents within 6 hours and keeping logs for 180 days in India. MeitY's India AI Governance Guidelines (November 2025) are voluntary today. The RBI FREE-AI framework sets expectations for banks and NBFCs, including a board-approved AI policy.

## UAE and GCC
The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) covers personal data used by AI on the mainland and in most free zones. DIFC Data Protection Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by autonomous and semi-autonomous systems: notice at first use, human-defined purposes, human-intervention triggers for biased outcomes, a register of use cases and, for high-risk processing, an Autonomous Systems Officer. ADGM has GDPR-style rules including automated decision-making rights. The UAE Charter for the Development and Use of AI sets voluntary principles. In Saudi Arabia, the PDPL applies to AI processing personal data, and SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles guide governance.

## United States
The US has no federal AI law. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) is the reference framework that buyers expect, and it maps well onto ISO/IEC 42001. State laws are growing: the Texas Responsible AI Governance Act (in force January 2026) treats NIST AI RMF compliance as a safe harbor; Colorado regulates automated decisions in areas such as employment and lending; New York City requires bias audits for automated hiring tools; and California has transparency rules for generative AI. Federal regulators such as the FTC, EEOC and CFPB apply existing law to AI.
