# ISO/IEC 42001 overview

## What ISO/IEC 42001 is
ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first certifiable international standard for an AI management system (AIMS). It tells an organization how to govern the AI it builds, provides or uses, in the same management-system style as ISO 9001 and ISO/IEC 27001. Supporting standards include ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management guidance), ISO/IEC 42005 (AI system impact assessment guidance, 2025) and ISO/IEC 42006 (requirements for bodies that certify an AIMS, 2025).

## Structure of the standard
Clauses 4 to 10 are mandatory requirements: context (4), leadership (5), planning including risk and impact assessment (6), support (7), operation (8), performance evaluation (9) and improvement (10). Annex A lists 38 reference controls grouped under 9 control areas, A.2 to A.10. Annex B gives implementation guidance for those controls. An organization may exclude an Annex A control only with a documented justification in its Statement of Applicability.

## The nine Annex A control areas
A.2 AI policies. A.3 Internal organization, including roles and a way to report concerns. A.4 Resources for AI systems (data, tools, compute, people). A.5 Assessing impacts of AI systems on individuals, groups and society. A.6 AI system lifecycle: design, verification and validation, deployment, monitoring, technical documentation and event logs. A.7 Data for AI systems: quality, provenance and preparation. A.8 Information for interested parties, including incident communication. A.9 Use of AI systems, including human oversight. A.10 Third-party and customer relationships, including supplier assessment.

## Certification process
Certification is done by an accredited certification body in two stages. Stage 1 is a readiness review of documents such as scope, AI policy, risk methodology, Statement of Applicability, internal audit report and management review minutes. Stage 2 tests whether the system works in practice through interviews and sampled records. Findings are graded as major nonconformities, minor nonconformities or opportunities for improvement. A certificate lasts three years, with surveillance audits in years one and two.

## Typical 90-day implementation plan
Phase 1 Mobilize (weeks 1–2): appoint a sponsor and AIMS manager, start the AI inventory, agree scope. Phase 2 Assess (weeks 3–5): approve the risk method, build the risk register and impact assessments. Phase 3 Build (weeks 6–9): implement controls, sign the Statement of Applicability, launch training. Phase 4 Prove (weeks 10–12): run metrics, a full internal audit and a management review, then book the Stage 1 audit.
