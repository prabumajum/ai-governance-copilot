# EU AI Act essentials

## Scope
The EU AI Act applies to anyone placing an AI system on the EU market or whose AI output is used in the EU, so companies in India, the UAE or the US that serve European customers can be in scope. Obligations depend on the organization's role: provider (develops or brands the system), deployer (uses it professionally), importer or distributor.

## Risk tiers
Prohibited practices include social scoring, manipulative techniques that cause harm, untargeted scraping of facial images, and emotion recognition in the workplace or in education (with narrow exceptions). High-risk systems include AI used in employment and recruitment, credit scoring, education and exams, access to essential public and private services, biometrics, critical infrastructure, law enforcement, migration and border control, and AI embedded in regulated products such as medical devices. Limited-risk or transparency obligations cover chatbots, emotion recognition, deepfakes and AI-generated content: people must be told they are interacting with AI and synthetic content must be marked. Everything else is minimal risk.

## Key dates
Prohibitions and the AI literacy duty have applied since 2 February 2025, and general-purpose AI model obligations since 2 August 2025. Transparency duties for AI interactions and generated content apply from August 2026. After the 2026 Digital Omnibus, stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III) apply from 2 December 2027 and high-risk AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028. Always check the current consolidated text before relying on a date.

## High-risk requirements
Providers of high-risk systems need a risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, automatic logging, transparency to deployers, human oversight, accuracy and robustness, a quality management system, conformity assessment and post-market monitoring with serious-incident reporting. Deployers must use systems according to instructions, assign human oversight, monitor and keep logs, and inform affected workers and people.

## Relationship with ISO/IEC 42001
An ISO/IEC 42001 certificate does not by itself prove AI Act conformity, but an AI management system builds most of the machinery the Act requires: risk management, data governance, documentation, human oversight and monitoring.

## Penalties
Fines can reach EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices, and EUR 15 million or 3% for most other breaches, with lower caps for SMEs.
