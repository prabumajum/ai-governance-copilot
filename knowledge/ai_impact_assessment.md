# AI system impact assessment

## Risk assessment versus impact assessment
A risk assessment asks what could go wrong for the organization. An impact assessment asks what the AI system could do to people, groups and society. ISO/IEC 42001 requires both (clause 6.1.4 and Annex A.5), and ISO/IEC 42005:2025 gives detailed guidance on impact assessment.

## When to run one
Screen every AI system and run a full assessment when any screening answer is yes. Does the system make or shape decisions about individuals, such as hiring, credit, pricing, access to services, education or health? Does it process sensitive or children's data? Could it affect someone's safety, legal rights or livelihood? Does it interact with the public without clear disclosure that it is AI? Would a law class it as high-risk? Could one error affect many people at once? Repeat the assessment when the system, its use, its data or the law changes, and at least yearly for high-impact systems.

## Impact assessment template
A full assessment records: system description and purpose; affected parties, including vulnerable groups; intended and foreseeable uses, including misuse; potential impacts on fairness, privacy, safety, autonomy and transparency; severity and likelihood using the same scales as the risk method; mitigations such as human oversight, notices and appeal routes; the residual impact and the decision (proceed, proceed with conditions, or do not deploy); what is communicated to affected people; and the next review date.

## Worked example: CV screening tool
A vendor CV screening tool used by HR may rank down candidates with employment gaps, causing indirect discrimination against older applicants or people returning from career breaks. Mitigations: obtain the vendor's bias-test results per protected group, have HR review rejected CVs above an experience threshold, tell applicants AI is used and offer human review, and run a quarterly adverse-impact analysis. Decision: proceed with conditions, approved by the AI governance committee, review in six months.

## Combining with privacy assessments
Where personal data is involved, combine the AI impact assessment with the privacy impact assessment required by data protection law (a DPIA under GDPR or India's DPDP Act for significant data fiduciaries) and, in the EU, the fundamental rights impact assessment where required. One combined assessment avoids duplicated work.
